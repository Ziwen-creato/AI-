腾讯提供补贴，OpenClaw 在中国得到广泛采用

Crypto Briefing2026-08-10生成于 2026-08-11 17:41

这款开源人工智能代理已成为 GitHub 增长最快的项目之一，中国地方政府提供了数百万美元的补贴以加速其整合。

由奥地利程序员构建的开源人工智能代理不知何故成为中国最热门的科技痴迷。 OpenClaw 可以自主处理网页浏览、电子邮件管理和预订服务等任务，它正在全国范围内传播，其速度甚至让其最大的支持者措手不及。

2026 年 3 月，科技巨头腾讯和百度在深圳和北京举办了公开设置活动，吸引了从大学生到退休人员的数百名参加者。地方政府正在为这一趋势投入大量资金，深圳龙岗区为基于 OpenClaw 构建的应用程序提供高达 1000 万元人民币（约合 140 万美元）的补贴。

OpenClaw 由 Peter Steinberger 创建，并迅速成为 GitHub 增长最快的存储库之一。它的功能就像一个数字助理，不仅可以回答问题，还可以在您的计算机上实际执行操作：浏览网站、填写表格、发送消息、进行预订。

字节跳动和阿里巴巴围绕该工具迅速构建了生态系统，构建了插入开源框架的集成和服务。与此同时，科技中心以外的城市也开始采取行动。无锡宣布专门为利用 OpenClaw 的制造应用提供高达 500 万元人民币（69 万美元）的补贴，而合肥也启动了自己的资助计划。

根据 SecurityScorecard 指标，中国在 OpenClaw 的采用方面现已超过美国。

这种病毒般的吸引力也围绕该工具产生了自己的模因文化。 OpenClaw 自主完成复杂的多步骤任务的视频在中国社交媒体上广泛传播，将通常是一种小众开发工具变成了一种更接近消费者现象的东西。

网上流传着这样的说法：如果中国工人不每天使用 OpenClaw，他们将面临解雇。研究表明，雇主尚未确认强制实施。用户自愿选择与该平台互动。

中国多个城市的地方政府宣布了旨在加速人工智能工具集成的补贴计划，OpenClaw 是主要受益者。这符合中国到2030年推动技术进步的更广泛的国家战略。

资金不是直接资助 OpenClaw 的开发，而是流向在其上构建应用程序的公司和开发人员。就背景而言，像龙岗这样的一个区的 1000 万元补贴足以资助多个全职开发 OpenClaw 相关产品的初创团队。

中央当局对 OpenClaw 的数据访问能力发出了警报。中国监管机构已发布安全警告，这些警告已经影响到某些行业的部署，特别是政府机构、金融机构系统和国防相关产业。

可用性挑战也依然存在。设置它仍然需要技术知识，而且不同的中国网络服务和平台的可靠性各不相同。腾讯和百度主办的公共安装活动在一定程度上就是为了解决这个问题，引导较少的技术用户完成安装和配置。

英文原文全文

The open-source AI agent has become one of GitHub's fastest-growing projects, with Chinese local governments offering millions in subsidies to accelerate its integration. An open-source AI agent built by an Austrian programmer has somehow become China’s hottest tech obsession. OpenClaw, which autonomously handles tasks like web browsing, email management, and booking services, is spreading across the country at a pace that has caught even its biggest proponents off guard. In March 2026, tech giants Tencent and Baidu hosted public setup events in Shenzhen and Beijing that drew hundreds of attendees ranging from university students to retirees. Local governments are throwing real money at the trend, with Shenzhen’s Longgang district offering subsidies of up to 10 million yuan (roughly $1.4 million) for applications built on top of OpenClaw. OpenClaw was created by Peter Steinberger and quickly became one of GitHub’s fastest-growing repositories. It functions as a digital assistant that doesn’t just answer questions but actually does things on your computer: navigating websites, filling out forms, sending messages, making reservations. ByteDance and Alibaba have rapidly built ecosystems around the tool, constructing integrations and services that plug into the open-source framework. Meanwhile, cities beyond the tech hubs are getting in on the action. Wuxi announced subsidies of up to 5 million yuan ($690,000) specifically for manufacturing applications that leverage OpenClaw, while Hefei has launched its own funding programs. According to SecurityScorecard metrics, China has now outpaced the United States in OpenClaw adoption. The viral appeal has also generated its own meme culture around the tool. Videos of OpenClaw autonomously completing complex multi-step tasks have circulated widely on Chinese social media, turning what would normally be a niche developer tool into something closer to a consumer phenomenon. Claims have circulated online that Chinese workers face termination if they don’t use OpenClaw daily. Research indicates there are no confirmed mandatory implementations by employers. Users are voluntarily choosing to engage with the platform. Local governments across multiple Chinese cities have announced subsidy programs designed to accelerate AI tool integration, with OpenClaw as a primary beneficiary. This fits into China’s broader national strategy to promote technological advancement by 2030. Rather than funding OpenClaw’s development directly, the money flows to companies and developers building applications on top of it. For context, 10 million yuan in subsidies from a single district like Longgang is enough to fund multiple startup teams working full-time on OpenClaw-adjacent products. Central authorities have raised alarms about OpenClaw’s data access capabilities. Chinese regulators have issued security warnings that are already affecting deployment in certain sectors, particularly government agencies, financial institutions, and defense-adjacent industries. Usability challenges also persist. Setting it up still requires technical knowledge, and reliability across different Chinese web services and platforms varies. The public setup events hosted by Tencent and Baidu were partly designed to address this, walking less technical users through installation and configuration.
