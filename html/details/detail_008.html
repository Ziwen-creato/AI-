环游图书世界：2026 年 8 月 10 日

Publishingperspectives.com2026-08-10生成于 2026-08-11 17:42

本周，主编安德鲁·理查德·阿尔巴内塞 (Andrew Richard Albanese) 代表出版分析师卡洛·卡伦霍 (Carlo Carrenho) 对国际出版界的头条新闻进行了回顾。

作者：Andrew Richard Albanese，主编

与此同时，本周的头条新闻包括：第三家俄罗斯出版商因“LGBTQ+极端主义”被判刑；巴西对国际化的关注似乎正在取得回报；哈珀柯林斯 (Harper Collins) 报告称，第四季度业绩强劲，财年稳健，并希望人工智能能够帮助实现未来更强劲的业绩；据说土耳其的图书馆正在经历巨大的增长。

据报道，现已倒闭的俄罗斯独立出版商 Popcorn Books 前负责人德米特里·普罗托波波夫 (Dmitry Protopopov) 在因 LGBTQ+“极端主义”指控被定罪后被判处四年缓刑。报告指出，普罗托波波夫是 2025 年 5 月被捕的三人之一，此前俄罗斯最高法院于 2023 年裁定所谓的“国际 LGBT 运动”为非法，将在俄罗斯出版、出售或传播 LGBTQ+ 主题的文学作品定为犯罪行为。

报告称：“检察官指控普罗托波波夫和他的同事设计了一项计划，允许他们向‘忠实客户’出售 LGBTQ+ 主题的书籍，以避免冲销 1000 万卢布（121,700 美元）的损失。”据报道，除了缓刑外，法院还命令普罗托波波夫缓刑五年；禁止他从事出版或开发工作数字媒体部门；并没收了他在俄罗斯最大出版集团 Eksmo 工作期间赚到的所有钱，该集团于 2023 年收购了 Popcorn Books。

在此消息发布之前，今年夏天早些时候，另外两名被检察官指控的人在达成认罪协议后收到了几乎相同的判决，据 Mediazona 的一份令人不寒而栗的报道称，其中包括作证指控 Eksmo 高管，其中包括首席执行官叶夫根尼·卡佩耶夫 (Evgeny Kapyev)。正如本专栏之前分享的那样，卡皮耶夫曾被短暂拘留并于四月获释。

据报道，8 月 5 日俄罗斯的一次袭击摧毁了乌克兰出版社 BookChef 的另外 10 万本书，据说这是该出版社剩余的库存。 7 月，俄罗斯之前对基辅的一次袭击摧毁了大约 80 万本 BookChef 书籍。  该出版商在其社交媒体上的一份声明中表示：“这是我们所有剩余的仓库库存和最近才从印刷厂运来的新版本，很快就会到达您的手中。”

最新的攻击发生在乌克兰出版基础设施似乎受到蓄意、持续的攻击之际。 “过去四年里，乌克兰平均每年出版约 2500 万册图书。仅在 2026 年 7 月，俄罗斯的袭击就毁坏了超过 150 万册乌克兰图书。这意味着仅今年夏天，乌克兰就损失了超过三分之一的年度图书产量，”Chytomo 报告道。

据报道，布拉格将举办库尔德和阿拉伯图书展博览会，定于 8 月 15 日至 16 日举行。该活动是与捷克笔会俱乐部合作举办的，“将出版商、作家、翻译家和读者聚集在一起，进行为期两天的文学讨论、新书发布会和文化活动，旨在加强库尔德、阿拉伯和欧洲社区之间的文化交流”，该活动将纪念已故库尔德作家穆罕默德·赛义德·侯赛因（于 2022 年去世），表彰他对库尔德文学和文化的“持久贡献”。

捷克笔会主席吉日·德德切克 (Jiří Dědeček) 表示：“这次博览会是对书面文字及其拉近文化和人民联系能力的庆祝。”他将这次博览会描述为对文学将不同文化的人们联系起来的能力的庆祝。总部位于伦敦的拉米纳图书公司 (Ramina Books) 总监海瑟姆·侯赛因 (Haitham Hussein) 也将参加此次展会，他表示，此次展会是“在出版、翻译和发行领域发展新合作伙伴关系”的宝贵机会。组织者表示，他们希望将阿拉伯和库尔德书展打造成“布拉格文化日历上的永久固定项目”。

报告称，巴西出版商旨在更好地“国际化”巴西出版的举措的数据显示，2026 年上半年世界领先的文学博览会上的国际业务大幅增长。报告指出，翻译补助金在交易中“发挥了重要作用”，“成为外国出版商将巴西出版纳入其中的决定性激励因素”。n 作品在他们的目录中。”此外，报告指出，“现场参加贸易展览非常重要”。

巴西图书商会主席塞瓦尼·马托斯告诉媒体，这种增长“证明全球市场不仅想要消费巴西印刷的实体书，而且积极希望将我们的声音和故事翻译并纳入他们自己的语言和目录中。”报告指出，巴西的国际议程将于 2026 年 10 月的法兰克福书展上继续，届时巴西将拥有自己的展位和会议，“重点关注版权销售和网络”，并将参加阿拉伯联合酋长国的沙迦出版商大会和墨西哥的瓜达拉哈拉国际书展。

据《出版商周刊》报道，哈珀柯林斯公司迎来了一个稳健的财年，第四季度业绩强劲，收入增长了 15%。根据一份新闻稿，哈珀柯林斯公司的总销售额在截至 6 月 30 日的财年中增长了 6%，达到 22.9 亿美元。然而，利润下降了 3%，至 2.87 亿美元。 Harper 的代表表示，利润下降的原因是图书馆供应商 Baker & Taylor 于 2025 年秋季倒闭，导致“1300 万美元的冲销”，以及“与 HarperCollins 国际业务库存相关的 1600 万美元的冲销”。全年数字图书销量增长 4%，其中有声读物销量增长 16%。

尼曼实验室报告称，2026 年普利策奖获得者和入围者中有 15 个新闻类别gories（于 5 月 4 日宣布）“五名获奖者和三名决赛入围者在其提交的材料中披露了人工智能的采用情况……这是自 2024 年添加披露要求以来最多的一次。”

普利策奖负责人马乔里·米勒 (Marjorie Miller) 告诉尼曼实验室：“显而易见，人工智能也许会继续存在。” “业界（过去）对人工智能工具的担忧远比现在要严重，现在人们更清楚地了解什么用途可能是合适的——例如数据收集和分析——什么时候可能不合适，例如以任何可能被考虑获得普利策奖的格式撰写和编辑故事。”米勒警告说，随着人工智能的发展，“记者将需要向普利策奖‘确保和保证’，提交的内容最终是由人类制作的，即使人工智能被用作辅助工具。”

本文阐述了人工智能协助记者工作的几种方式。但文章补充道，考虑到“最近关于人工智能生成的文本据称出现在获奖文学作品中的争议”，米勒表示，明年的普利策奖将在“他们的图书参赛表格中纳入人工智能披露问题”。

据《自由报日报》报道，土耳其的图书馆“顾客数量、会员数量和实体扩张都创历史新高”。文化和旅游部长穆罕默德·努里·埃尔索伊 (Mehmet Nuri Ersoy) 在最近开放的Şırnak省公共图书馆上公布了这些数字，透露土耳其的“图书馆服务总面积”在八年内几乎增加了两倍，增加了面积从32万平方米减少到近90万平方米。报告补充道：“他补充说，同期公共图书馆的会员数量从 280 万增加到 720 万，而用户数量从 2800 万增加到 3920 万。”

埃尔索伊在推动扩张过程中提到了政府的“全面投资”，以“确保土耳其各地的儿童和年轻人更容易获得图书馆服务”，并指出图书馆正在超越人们获取图书的地方，转变为“为土耳其社会、文化、科学和经济发展做出贡献的生产和机会中心”。他还提到了数字图书馆服务的扩展，指出有78个“移动图书馆为那些无法使用固定图书馆设施的人提供服务”。

Index on Censorship 宣布将于 9 月在英国禁书周之前为英国和美国图书馆员举办一场虚拟的“跨大西洋对话”。

“这次谈话不是回顾过去，而是提出了一个更紧迫的问题：未来五年对我们的要求是什么，我们该如何准备？”一份新闻稿写道。该会议面向图书馆员，并将提供捍卫阅读自由的“积极主动的路线图”。 “虽然图书审查制度经常被描述为北美危机，但这次会议对英国来说既是战略警告，又是战术指南。”  虚拟活动定于 9 月 14 日举行，感兴趣的各方可以在这里注册。

Editor-in-Chief Andrew Richard Albanese stands in this week for publishing analyst Carlo Carrenho with a review of the headlines from across the international publishing world. By Andrew Richard Albanese, Editor-in-Chief In the meantime, among the headlines this week: a third Russian publisher has been sentenced for “LGBTQ+ extremism”; Brazil’s focus on internationalization appears to be paying off; HarperCollins reports a strong fourth quarter and a solid fiscal year, and is looking to AI to help deliver stronger results going forward; and Türkiye’s libraries are said to be experiencing massive growth. reports that Dmitry Protopopov, former head of now defunct Russian indie publisher Popcorn Books, has received a four-year suspended sentence after being convicted on charges of LGBTQ+ “extremism.” The report notes that Protopopov was one of three people arrested in May 2025 in the wake of a 2023 Russian Supreme Court ruling that outlawed the so-called “international LGBT movement,” making it a crime to publish, sell, or distribute literature with LGBTQ+ themes in Russia. “Prosecutors accused Protopopov and his colleagues of devising a scheme that would allow them to sell books with LGBTQ+ themes to ‘loyal customers’ in an effort to avoid writing off 10 million rubles ($121,700) in losses,” the report states. In addition to his suspended prison sentence, the court reportedly ordered Protopopov to serve five years on probation; banned him from working in the publishing or digital media sectors; and seized all of the money he earned while employed by Eksmo, Russia’s largest publishing group, which acquired Popcorn Books in 2023. The news comes after two others charged by prosecutors received nearly identical sentences after striking plea deals earlier this summer, which, according to a chilling report in Mediazona, included testifying against Eksmo executives, including CEO Evgeny Kapyev. As this column previously shared, Kapyev was briefly detained and released in April. reports that an August 5 Russian attack destroyed another 100,000 books belonging to the Ukrainian publishing house BookChef, said to be the remainder of the publisher’s remaining stock. In July, a previous Russian attack on Kyiv destroyed approximately 800,000 BookChef books.  “This was all of our remaining warehouse stock and new releases that had only recently arrived from printing houses and were soon supposed to be in your hands,” the publisher said, in a statement on its social media. The latest attack comes amid what appears to be a deliberate, sustained assault on Ukraine’s publishing infrastructure. “Over the past four years, an average of around 25 million book copies were published annually in Ukraine. Russian attacks destroyed more than 1.5 million Ukrainian book copies in July (2026) alone. This means that this summer alone, Ukraine has lost more than one-third of its annual book output,” Chytomo reports. reports that  Prague will host the Kurdish and Arab Book Fair, set for August 15–16. “Bringing together publishers, writers, translators, and readers for two days of literary discussions, book launches, and cultural events aimed at strengthening cultural exchange between the Kurdish, Arab, and European communities,” the event is being organized is in cooperation with the Czech PEN Club, and will be dedicated to the late Kurdish writer Mohammad Sayyid Hussein, who died in 2022, recognizing his “lasting contribution” to Kurdish literature and culture. “This fair is a celebration of the written word and its ability to bring cultures and peoples closer together,” said Jiří Dědeček, President of the Czech PEN Club, who described the fair as a celebration of literature’s ability to connect people across cultures. Haitham Hussein, Director of London-based Ramina Books, which will be at the fair, agrees, saying that said the fair represents a valuable opportunity to “develop new partnerships in publishing, translation, and distribution.” Organizers said they hope to establish the Arab and Kurdish Book Fair as “a permanent fixture on Prague’s cultural calendar.” reports that data from a Brazilian Publishers initiative to better “internationalize” Brazilian publishing showed a significant jump in international business at the world’s leading literary fairs in the first half of 2026. The report notes that translation grants “played an important role” in dealmaking, “serving as a decisive incentive for foreign publishers to include Brazilian works in their catalogs.” Furthermore, “on-site participation at trade shows makes all the difference,” the report notes. Brazilian Book Chamber president Sevani Matos told the outlet that the growth “proves that the global market not only wants to consume physical books printed in Brazil, but actively desires to translate and incorporate our voices and stories into their own languages ​​and catalogs.” The Brazilian international agenda will continue in 2026 at the Frankfurt Book Fair in October, the report notes, where Brazil will have its own stand and meetings “focused on rights sales and networking,” as well as participation in the Sharjah Publishers Conference in the United Arab Emirates, and the Guadalajara International Book Fair in Mexico. Publishers Weekly reports that HarperCollins turned in a solid fiscal year paced by a strong fourth quarter, in which revenue rose 15%. According to a release, total sales at HarperCollins increased 6% in the fiscal year ended June 30, to $2.29 billion. Profits, however, fell 3%, to $287 million. Harper reps said the dip in profits was due to a “$13 million write-off” from the closure of library supplier Baker & Taylor, which collapsed in fall 2025, as well as “a $16 million write-off related to inventory of HarperCollins’s international operations.” Digital sales rose 4% for the full year, led by a 16% gain in audiobook sales. Nieman Lab reports that among the 2026 Pulitzer Prize winners and finalists across 15 journalism categories (which were announced on May 4) “five winners and three finalists disclosed AI adoption in their submissions… the most since the disclosure requirement was added in 2024.” “To state the obvious, perhaps, AI is here to stay,” Marjorie Miller, the administrator of the Pulitzer Prizes, told Nieman Lab. “The industry [used to be] far more apprehensive about AI tools than it is today, with a clearer understanding now of what uses might be appropriate—data collection and analysis, for example—and when it might not, such as in writing and editing stories in any format that might be considered for a Pulitzer Prize.” Miller cautioned that, as AI evolves, “reporters will need to ‘ensure and reassure’ the Pulitzers that submissions are ultimately produced by human beings, even when AI is used as an assistive tool.” The article illustrates several ways AI is being used to assist the work of journalists. But given “recent controversies over AI-generated text allegedly appearing in prize-winning literary works,” the article adds, Miller said next year’s Pulitzers will include “an AI disclosure question in their book entry forms.” Hurriyet Daily reports that Türkiye’s libraries are seeing “record-high patron numbers, memberships, and physical expansion.” Culture and Tourism Minister Mehmet Nuri Ersoy announced the figures at the recent opening of the Şırnak Provincial Public Library, revealing that “the total library service area” in Türkiye has nearly tripled in eight years, increasing to nearly 900,000 square meters from 320,000 square meters. “He added that the number of members of public libraries rose from 2.8 million to 7.2 million during the same period, while the number of users increased from 28 million to 39.2 million,” the report adds. Driving the expansion, Ersoy cited “comprehensive investments” by the government to “ensure that children and young people across Türkiye have easier access to library services,” noting that libraries are moving beyond places where people access books, transforming into “production and opportunity centers that contribute to Türkiye’s social, cultural, scientific and economic development.” He also pointed to the expansion of digital library services, pointing out that there are 78 “mobile libraries providing services to those who cannot access fixed library facilities.” Index on Censorship has announced that it will host a virtual “transatlantic dialogue” for U.K. and U.S. librarians in September, ahead of Banned Books Week U.K. “Rather than looking back, this conversation asks a more urgent question: what will the next five years demand of us, and how can we prepare?” a release reads. The session is intended for librarians and will offer “a proactive roadmap” for defending the freedom to read. “While book censorship is often framed as a North American crisis, this session serves as both a strategic warning and a tactical guide for the U.K.”  The virtual event is set for September 14, and interested parties can register here.
