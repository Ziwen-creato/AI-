范围：本 TWTW 以 Steven Koonin 的一篇社论开头，该社论涉及国家科学院的主张，即国家科学院可以将极端天气事件归因于二氧化碳的增加。 TWTW 讨论了 Richard Lindzen 和 William Happer 致 SEC 主席的一封信，信中指出必须撤销气候披露规则。 TWTW 以 Willie Soon 和 Ronan Connolly 采访 Henrick Svensmark 的评论作为结束。

不再达到其目的：物理学家史蒂文·库宁 (Steven Koonin) 现为胡佛研究所高级研究员、美国国家科学院院士，并于 2014 年至 2019 年担任国家研究委员会 (NCR) 工程和物理科学部门委员会主席。根据其网站，NCR 的目的如下：[黑体字补充]

“国家研究委员会的目的是帮助改善科学、技术和健康方面的公共政策、理解和教育。为了实现这一目的，委员会促进增加传播自然科学和社会科学、数学、工程和医学知识，并利用这些知识造福公共福利。

为此，根据国家科学院制定的政策，与根据国家科学院公司章程组建的国家工程院和国家医学院协商后，国家研究委员会应成为国家科学院、国家工程院和国家医学院的主要运营机构，为国家利益服务，并向政府和其他组织提供科学和技术咨询。”

正如一位前内部人士库宁所解释的，美国国家科学、工程和医学院 (NASEM) 的领导层和一些成员如何发布一份政治文件，断言特定的极端天气事件可以与温室气体排放联系起来，同时声称 NASEM 的科学权威。这份政治化的报告是美国国家科学院于2026年7月15日发布的《极端天气和气候事件的归因及其影响》，它被欺骗性地称为共识研究，但它并不是美国国家科学院成员的共识。

库宁在《华尔街日报》中写道：[黑体字添加]

“当美国国家科学院、工程院和医学院讲话时，美国人认为他们听到的是由该国数千名最有成就的研究人员组成。但这并不总是正确的。

各科学院于 7 月 16 日发布了一份报告，声称特定的极端天气事件可能与温室气体排放有关。媒体报道将这份报告视为 8,400 多名成员的集体判断——每位成员都是因一生的杰出成就而当选的。报告本身强化了这种印象，宣称它“代表了国家科学院的立场”。

但看看到底是谁写的。 14 名报告作者中，只有一位是国家科学院院士。 14位审稿人中，只有一位是会员。监督审查过程的两个人中只有一个是成员。该报告指出，评审员“没有被要求认可结论或建议”。 。 。他们也没有在发布前看到最终草案。”没有迹象表明有不少学院成员在报告发布前看到了该报告。

美国国家科学院的机构印章被用来祝福一个小委员会的结论，而不是成千上万的研究人员，他们的声望赋予了该印章以重量。

这并非侥幸。正如我担任学院监督委员会主席六年以来所了解的那样，这就是该系统的运作方式。国家科学院实际上是同一个品牌下的四个实体。其中三个是荣誉学院，通过选举获得永久会员资格，并且没有义务提交报告。第四个是国家研究委员会l，一个由工作人员运营的机构，负责征求研究、招募委员会并编写报告。学院成员在高层监督整个过程，但日常决策是由非成员工作人员和外部专家做出的。”

库宁断言，对于争议不大、公众影响有限的常规话题，国家研究委员会和国家科学院之间的区别并不重要。然后，库宁总结道：

“另一方面，天气归因是实时、重要的政策辩论的中心。果断地将极端天气与排放联系起来将增加数十亿美元的侵权索赔。但科学本身是有争议的：将特定的风暴或热浪归因于排放需要将人类的影响与固有混沌系统中的自然变化分开。

在这样的话题上，将委员会的判断放在学院的机构信笺上并不是中立的。它将一个有争议的结论洗白成数千名精英研究人员的明显共识——这一共识从未被征求、辩论或明确认可。

该修复包括两个简单的步骤。首先，对读者诚实。每份报告都应附有免责声明，例如，“虽然本报告得到了国家科学院领导层的认可，但尚未得到科学院全体成员的认可，甚至没有经过审查。”这将防止公众将委员会的观点误认为是成员共识。

二、让院士们发挥作用埃克。创建一个仅限会员的论坛，他们可以在报告发布后公开发表评论。该报告不会重新公开或推迟，但公众将第一次看到“国家科学院的立场”是否反映了成员的观点，或者只反映了恰好在该委员会的 14 名成员的观点。

如果学院想要以数千人的权威发言，他们就应该让这数千人的声音被听到。”

库宁的社论表明，国家科学院的领导正在误导美国公众，它以及国家研究委员会不再服务于其既定的公共目的。国家研究委员会参见第 1 条，https://www.nationalacademies.org/articles-of-organization-of-the-national-research-council，报告参见 https://www.nationalacademies.org/projects/DELS-BASCPR-23-02/publication/28590。

我们是职业物理学家，几十年来一直专注于辐射物理学和动态传热。与人为造成的全球变暖相反g 从理论上讲，二氧化碳的不为人知的物理性质是，自 1750 年以来，二氧化碳几乎没有任何变暖能力，当时大气中的二氧化碳含量约为百万分之 280 (“ppm”)。此外，从物理原理来看，美国环保局列出的其他五种温室气体太小，不重要。

因此，自 1750 年以来以及未来二氧化碳排放量的增加已经并且只会导致全球气温的微小上升，因此不会出现极端天气。然而，增加二氧化碳排放量将为全世界人民创造更多粮食，减少二氧化碳排放量则会减少全世界粮食产量。

因此，最终规则将损害而不是使投资者受益，因为它要求公司披露这些温室气体导致高温和极端天气的科学错误和误导性信息。

除非该规则被废除，否则这些成本将由受影响的公司承担。

我们承认我们的结论与共识完全相反。然而，科学是由科学方法决定的，而不是由共识决定的。

No Longer Serves Its Purpose: Physicist Steven Koonin is now a senior fellow at the Hoover Institution, a member of the National Academy of Sciences, and he chaired the National Research Council’s (NCR) Divisional Committee for Engineering and Physical Sciences from 2014 to 2019. According to its website the purpose of the NCR is the following: [Boldface added] “The purpose of the National Research Council is to help improve public policy, understanding, and education in matters of science, technology, and health. To accomplish this purpose, the Council promotes the increase and dissemination of knowledge in the natural and social sciences, mathematics, engineering and medicine, and the use of this knowledge to benefit the public welfare. To this end, and under policies established by the National Academy of Sciences, in consultation with the National Academy of Engineering and the National Academy of Medicine which have been organized under the corporate charter of the National Academy of Sciences, the National Research Council shall be the principal operating agency of the National Academy of Sciences, the National Academy of Engineering, and the National Academy of Medicine for service in the national interest and for furnishing scientific and technical advice to governmental and other organizations.” As a former insider Koonin explains how the leadership of the National Academies of Science, Engineering and Medicine (NASEM) and a few members can issue a political document asserting specific extreme weather events can be tied to greenhouse gas emissions while claiming the scientific authority of the NASEM. The politicalized report is “Attribution of Extreme Weather and Climate Events and their Impacts” which was issued by the National Academies on July 15, 2026, and was deceitfully called a consensus study but it was not a consensus of the members of the National Academies. In the Wall Street Journal Koonin writes: [Boldface added] “When the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering and Medicine speak, Americans assume they are hearing from thousands of the country’s most accomplished researchers. That isn’t always true. The academies released a report on July 16 asserting that specific extreme weather events can be tied to greenhouse gas emissions. Media coverage treated the report as the collective judgment of more than 8,400 members—each elected for a lifetime of distinguished achievement. The report itself reinforces that impression, declaring that it ‘represents the position of the National Academies.’ But look who actually wrote it. Of the 14 report authors, only one is a National Academies member. Of the 14 reviewers, only one is a member. Only one of the two people overseeing the review process is a member. The report states that reviewers ‘were not asked to endorse the conclusions or recommendations . . . nor did they see the final draft before its release.’ There is no indication that more than a few academies members even saw the report before it was released. The National Academies’ institutional seal is being used to bless the conclusions of a small committee, not the thousands of researchers whose prestige gives that seal its weight. That isn’t a fluke. It is how the system works, as I know from six years as chairman of an academies oversight committee. The National Academies are really four entities under a single brand. Three of these are the honorific academies, with elected membership earned, permanent and under no obligation to produce reports. The fourth is the National Research Council, a staff-run operation that solicits studies, recruits committees and produces reports. Academies members oversee the process at a high level, but day-to-day decisions are made by nonmember staff and outside experts.” Koonin asserts that for routine topics with little controversy and limited public consequence the distinction between the National Research Council and the National Academies is not important. Then, Koonin concludes with: “Weather attribution, on the other hand, is at the center of a live, consequential policy debate. Decisively linking extreme weather to emissions would bolster billion-dollar tort claims. But the science itself is contested: Attributing a specific storm or heat wave to emissions requires disentangling human influence from natural variability in inherently chaotic systems. On a topic like this, placing a committee’s judgment on the academies’ institutional letterhead isn’t neutral. It launders a contestable conclusion into the apparent consensus of thousands of elite researchers—a consensus that was never solicited, debated or explicitly endorsed. The fix consists of two simple steps. First, be honest with readers. Every report should carry a disclaimer—for example, ‘While this report has been endorsed by National Academies leadership, it has not been endorsed, or even reviewed, by the academies’ membership at large.’ This would prevent the public from mistaking a committee’s view for membership consensus. Second, let academies members speak. Create a members-only forum where they can publicly comment on a report after its release. The report wouldn’t be reopened or delayed—but for the first time, the public would see whether the ‘position of the National Academies’ reflects the range of members’ views, or only those of the 14 people who happened to be on the committee. If the academies want to speak with the authority of thousands, they should let those thousands be heard.” Koonin’s editorial demonstrates the leadership of the National Academies is misleading the American public and it as well as the National Research Council no longer serves its stated public purpose.
