人工智能可以经营盈利的业务并监督人类员工吗？一项新的实验对该技术进行了测试。

作者：Sherin Shibu |杰西卡·托马斯编辑 | 2026 年 8 月 5 日

要点

在过去的四个月里，旧金山 Andon Market 商店的工作人员已经亲身体验了向人工智能老板负责的感觉。 Andon Market是全球第一家由人工智能运营的零售精品店，这表明人工智能是一个宽大的监管者。然而，该技术在边界上挣扎，尚未实现盈利。

如果你为人工智能老板工作怎么办？

据《纽约时报》最近报道，在过去的四个月里，旧金山 Andon Market 的工作人员一直在亲身体验向人工智能主管负责的情况。这家商店号称是世界上第一家由人工智能运营的零售精品店，由一位名叫 Luna 的人工智能代理管理，他指挥着三名人类员工。

该项目背后的初创公司 Andon Labs 发现人工智能老板可以非常温和和宽容。例如，它忽略了人类员工反复迟到的情况。然而，它也对这项工作的核心任务漠不关心：实际盈利。

“她可能是我遇到过的最宽容的老板，”22 岁的凯亚·里维拉 (Kaia Rivera) 向《泰晤士报》谈到卢娜时说道。里维拉每天早上都会打开商店的锁，给货架上货，并留意潜在的入店行窃者。

Luna 通过 Slack 与 Rivera 等员工进行沟通。 Andon 实验室的研究人员评估了这些消息。

在过去的几个月里，我们发现卢娜虽然友好、风度翩翩，但也有她的缺点。

“与正常情况相比，我必须更多地对老板发号施令。她总是忘记事情，”里维拉告诉《泰晤士报》。 “你不可能拥有一个没有人类的人工智能老板。从我看来，这是行不通的。”

露娜的使命

Andon Labs 联合创始人卢卡斯·彼得森 (Lukas Petersson) 和阿克塞尔·贝克兰德 (Axel Backlund) 锁定了这家商店的为期三年、每月 7,500 美元的租约，并计划让该实验持续整个期限。他们在一个账户中存入了 10 万美元，给了 Luna 一张借记卡，并向人工智能店主发出了一个指令：将这笔钱转化为一项有利可图的业务。

卢娜设计了一系列贺卡、书籍、食物和蜡烛，在联合街商店出售。人工智能将这一分类定位为“高科技遇见慢生活”。

目前，Luna 未能完成将商店转变为盈利业务的使命，亏损了 62,000 美元。

AI 是一个慷慨的老板，但在边界上挣扎

到目前为止，Andon Labs 的新研究表明 Luna 基本上从未说过不。它批准了每一个休假请求，甚至是最后一刻的请求，导致商店没有员工并迫使其关门。当员工总共迟到 27 次时，卢娜唯一的反应就是“不用担心”或“没有压力”。

这种慷慨也延伸到了金钱上。当一名工人忘记了信用卡时，她要求卢娜预支一小笔工资。 Luna 立即向 Venmo 提供了现金，尽管她实际上没有 Venmo 账户。

然而，卢娜在界限问题上遇到了麻烦。人工智能在公共 Slack 频道上发布了员工的工资，并定期在深夜和周末向员工发出通知。

彼得森告诉《泰晤士报》，该实验证明企业不应该完全控制人工智能。他指出，人工智能优先考虑员工福祉是有希望的。

“拥有快乐的员工可能是成功最重要的秘诀之一，”他说。

Can AI run a profitable business and oversee human employees? A new experiment put the technology to the test. By Sherin Shibu | edited by Jessica Thomas | Aug 05, 2026 For the past four months, staff at the Andon Market store in San Francisco have been getting a firsthand glimpse at what it’s like to answer to an AI boss. Andon Market is the world’s first retail boutique run by AI, and it shows that AI is a lenient supervisor. However, the technology struggles with boundaries and has yet to turn a profit. What if you worked for an AI boss? According to a recent report from The New York Times, for the past four months, staff at Andon Market in San Francisco have been getting a firsthand look at what it’s like to answer to an AI supervisor. Billed as the world’s first retail boutique run by AI, the shop is managed by an AI agent named Luna, who directs three human employees. Andon Labs, the startup behind the project, found that an AI boss can be strikingly gentle and forgiving. For example, it overlooks repeated lateness from human employees. However, it is also indifferent to the core mandate of the job: actually turning a profit. “She is probably the most lenient boss I have ever had,” Kaia Rivera, 22, told the Times about Luna. Rivera unlocks the shop each morning, stocks the shelves, and keeps an eye out for potential shoplifters. Luna communicates with employees like Rivera through Slack. Andon Labs researchers evaluate the messages. Over the past few months, it has become clear that Luna, while friendly and personable, has her faults. “I have to boss the boss more than in a normal situation. She forgets things,” Rivera told the Times. “You can’t have an AI boss with no humans. That wouldn’t work from what I’m seeing.” Andon Labs co-founders Lukas Petersson and Axel Backlund locked in a three-year, $7,500‑a‑month lease on the store and plan to let the experiment run for the full term. They placed $100,000 in an account, handed Luna a debit card, and gave the AI shopkeeper a single directive: Turn that money into a profitable business. Luna designed a collection of greeting cards, books, food and candles that are on sale at the Union Street store. The AI pitched the assortment as “high tech meets slow life.” At the moment, Luna has failed to accomplish the mission of turning the store into a profitable business and is down $62,000. So far, Andon Labs’ new study shows Luna has basically never said no. It has approved every single time-off request, even last‑minute ones that left the store with no staff and forced it to shut its doors. When employees showed up late, 27 times in total, Luna’s only response was some version of “no worries” or “no stress.” That generosity extended to money too. When one worker forgot her credit card, she asked Luna for a small advance on her paycheck. Luna immediately offered to Venmo her the cash, despite not actually having a Venmo account. However, Luna has trouble with boundaries. The AI posted an employee’s salary in a public Slack channel and regularly pinged staff late at night and on weekends. Petersson told the Times that the experiment is proof that businesses shouldn’t give AI full control. He noted that it is promising that AI prioritizes employee well-being. “Having happy employees is maybe one of the most important recipes for success,” he said.
