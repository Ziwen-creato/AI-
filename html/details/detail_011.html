网站的未来

Joost.blog2026-08-10生成于 2026-08-11 17:43

我的大部分工作时间都花在网站上：构建网站、优化网站，并帮助数百万人做同样的事情。因此，我最近不断回想起的问题是我整个领域的一个存在主义问题：网站将走向何方？五年后“网站”到底是什么？

有几件事同时成立，但其中一些却朝着不同的方向发展。但它们都指向一个目的地。该网站成为您业务的大本营：人类和代理都可以阅读它，对其进行操作，并检查您是否真实。

事情是这样的：网站从来都不是专门为人类构建的。我们一直在为人类和机器人构建。二十年来，我和其他人的建议都是：为人类构建，机器人就会效仿。这个建议是正确的，但只是因为历史的怪异。谷歌拥有数十亿美元和二十年的时间来非常非常擅长阅读适合人们的页面。

访问您网站的新一波代理则不具备这一点。坦率地说，大多数人工智能工具并不擅长弄清楚纯粹为人类眼球设计的页面上的内容。也许他们会到达那里。但至少在可预见的未来，您的网站需要易于代理解析和使用的界面。

SEO 突然更名为 GEO 和 AEO 也讲述了同样的故事。 SEO 始终是真正的“谷歌优化”。还有其他表面，也有针对它们优化的优秀 SEO，但 Google 的工作迄今为止投资回报率最高，所以这就是焦点所在。新名字主要反映一件事：我们现在必须针对多个表面进行优化。工作本身还是一如既往。改变的是，谷歌不再是唯一值得付出努力的表面。

最近的一个例子说明了这种转变的速度有多快。当 ChatGPT 搜索推出时，它将答案扎根于 Bing 的索引中，突然间每个人都争先恐后地想知道他们在 Bing 中的表现如何。他们总是应该知道的。但多年来，Bing 的投资回报率一直很低，所以没人在意。然后 ChatGPT 被发现悄悄拉取 Google 的搜索结果，而就在过去几周，Bing 再次出现在其检索流量中。两年内发生了三次变动，但都没有宣布。这就是一则轶事中的新现实：哪个表面重要不再由你决定，而且它可以在一夜之间改变。

在您急于为代理商建立一个单独的网站之前：我们之前已经这样做过。还记得 m-dot 网站吗？还记得AMP吗？网站的并行版本总是会不同步，然后就会消失。答案不是第二个网站。它是具有多个渲染的事实来源之一，其中机器友好的表面是根据与人类相同的内容生成的。

建立一个技术上正确的网站总是异常困难。您必须真正了解一个好的网站的规范：标准、边缘情况、底层的复杂性以及这些标准如何相互作用。很少有人这样做过。世界上最好的 SEO，仅此而已。

这种情况正在迅速改变。这正是为什么我建立了规范网站：将代理指向规范并让它疯狂。过去需要稀有专业知识的事情正在变成你可以运行的事情。

因此，卓越的技术不再是稀有技能；这是一个选择。这并不意味着每个人都会做出这样的选择，当然还不会，所以目前成为少数这样做的人之一仍然具有真正的优势。但方向很明确：门槛不断上升，过去赢得市场的东西慢慢变成了底线。持久的价值转移到机器无法回答你的问题上。网站对您的业务重要吗？你对此有什么真实的说法吗？

在我住的地方，一个擅长工作、能正常沟通的水管工、木匠或电工，无论有没有网站，都会全年忙碌。一个好的网站能让他们更加忙碌吗？大概。他们想要那个吗？许多人不这样做，这是一个完全理性的选择。

对于品牌来说，情况恰恰相反。该网站可能比他们可以开设的最别致的大街商店更重要。部分是为了体验，但主要是为了一个极其简单的原因：商业街只有这么多，而你的潜在买家却无处不在。

网站过去是 HTML 的东西，在某些情况下还带有 API。这已经不够了。如果您的网站对您很重要，那么它至少应该是一个 MCP 服务器，并且在许多情况下甚至还提供一个命令行客户端。这样代理就可以直接与您的企业互动：检查可用性、地点订单，预订时段。

具体标准将会发生变化。 MCP 就是今天的答案；之后还会有别的事情发生。持久的一点是：一个网站正在变成一堆界面。一种用于人类，多种用于机器，机器不仅回答问题，还采取行动。

越来越多的购买决策发生在远离您的网站的地方：在人工智能答案中、在聊天界面中、在代理介导的交易中。这是令人不舒服的一点。你可以愤怒地反对它，也可以为它做好计划。

该网站不会在那个世界中消亡。它的角色发生了转变。它成为有关您的业务的规范真相来源：记录系统，为做出实际决策的每个表面提供信息。如果人工智能助手推荐你，那是因为你的大本营给了它充分的理由。简单、陈旧、对机器不利的网站根本不被推荐。

有一点将所有这一切联系在一起：通用的人工智能技术将让你在这个世界上一事无成。每个人都可以无限量地生产它，这使得它毫无价值。唯一无法产生的就是真实：真实的工作、真实的人、真实的声音、真正为你担保的真实客户。

在代理世界中，真实性不能仅仅被声称，它必须是可检查的。在两个供应商之间进行选择的代理商可以便宜且即时地交叉参考所有内容。您网站上的故事与您在其他地方留下的线索相符吗？人真的存在吗？评论站得住脚吗？ SEO 会认识到 goal：这就是 Google 的 E-E-A-T 一直追求的目标。不同之处在于，谷歌通过信号来估计信任度；代理人只需去检查即可。真实性不再是一个营销词，而是成为基础设施：身份、出处、信任。证明你就是你所说的你即将成为网络中最有价值的一层。

您无法单独在自己的网站上构建该层：您还需要存在于网络上的其他位置，并且需要其他人为您提供担保。我们称之为链接建设、公关还是其他什么都不重要。这是必须要做的。证据不仅是技术性的：一个真正优秀的页面、真实的人、真实的面孔和真实的故事，也能起到同样的作用。它可能是您网站上最好的页面。

没死。甚至还差得远。但不同的是：该网站正在成为企业经过验证的、机器可读的大本营。它为所有其他表面提供营养。它回答代理并接受他们的命令。证明这个生意是真实的。有时，它仍然会被真人拜访，他们最好在到达时找到真实的东西。

在这个世界上蓬勃发展的网站做对了三件事。它们在技术上非常出色：现在是一种选择，而且目前仍然是一种优势。特工们很容易找到和阅读它们：管道问题。它们毫无疑问是真实的：困难的部分，而且一直都是。

英文原文全文

I’ve spent most of my working life on websites: building them, optimizing them, and helping millions of people do the same. So the question I keep coming back to lately is an existential one for my whole field: where are websites going? What even is “the website” five years from now? Several things are true at once, and some of them pull in different directions. But they point to one destination. The website becomes the home base of your business: humans and agents alike can read it, act on it, and check that you are real. Here’s the thing: websites were never built just for humans. We were always building for humans and bots. For twenty years, my advice, and everyone else’s, was: build for humans, and the bots will follow. That advice was correct, but only because of a quirk of history. Google had billions of dollars and two decades to get very, very good at reading pages meant for people. The new wave of agents visiting your website doesn’t have that. Most AI tools are, frankly, not that good at figuring out what’s on a page that was designed purely for human eyeballs. Maybe they’ll get there. But at least for the foreseeable future, your website needs surfaces that are easy for agents to parse and use. The sudden rebranding of SEO into GEO and AEO tells the same story. SEO was always really “Google optimization”. There were other surfaces, and good SEOs optimized for them too, but the Google work had by far the highest ROI, so that’s where the focus went. The new names mostly reflect one thing: we now have to optimize for more surfaces than one. The work itself is the same as it always was. What changed is that Google is no longer the only surface worth the effort. A recent example of how quickly this shifts. When ChatGPT search launched, it grounded its answers in Bing’s index, and suddenly everyone was scrambling to figure out how they were doing in Bing. They always should have known. But for years the ROI on Bing was low, so nobody bothered. Then ChatGPT was caught quietly pulling Google’s results instead, and just these past few weeks Bing showed up again in its retrieval traffic. Three swings in under two years, none of them announced. That’s the new reality in one anecdote: which surface matters is no longer your call, and it can change overnight. Before you rush off to build a separate site for agents: we’ve done that before. Remember m-dot sites? Remember AMP? Parallel versions of your website always drift out of sync, and then they die. The answer isn’t a second website. It’s one source of truth with multiple renderings, where the machine-friendly surface is generated from the same content the human one is. Building a technically correct website was always absurdly hard. You had to actually know the spec of a good website: the standards, the edge cases, the intricacies underneath, and how these standards interact. Very few people ever did. The best SEOs in the world, and that’s about it. That’s changing fast. This is exactly why I built specification.website: point an agent at the spec and let it go nuts. What used to require rare expertise is becoming something you can run. So technical excellence is no longer a rare skill; it’s a choice. That doesn’t mean everyone will make that choice, certainly not yet, so for the moment there is still real advantage in being one of the few who do. But the direction is clear: the bar keeps rising, and what used to win markets slowly becomes the baseline. The lasting value moves to the questions machines can’t answer for you. Does a website even matter for your business? Do you have anything real to say on it? Where I live, a plumber, carpenter or electrician who is good at the job and can communicate half decently will be busy year round, with or without a website. Could a good website make them even busier? Probably. Do they want that? Many don’t, and that’s a perfectly rational choice. For a brand, it’s the opposite. The website is probably more important than the most chic high street store they could open. Partly for the experience, but mostly for a brutally simple reason: there are only so many high streets, and your potential buyers live everywhere. A website used to be an HTML thing, in some cases with an API. That’s no longer enough. If your website matters to you, it should at the very least also be an MCP server, and in many cases even ship a command-line client. That way agents can interact with your business directly: check availability, place the order, book the slot. The specific standards will churn. MCP is today’s answer; something else will come after it. The durable point is this: a website is becoming a bundle of interfaces. One for humans, several for machines, and the machine ones don’t just answer questions, they take actions. More and more buying decisions happen away from your site: in AI answers, in chat interfaces, in agent-mediated transactions. This is the uncomfortable one. You can rage against it or you can plan for it. The website doesn’t die in that world. Its role shifts. It becomes the canonical source of truth about your business: the system of record that feeds every surface where the actual decision gets made. If an AI assistant recommends you, it’s because your home base gave it every reason to. Thin, stale, machine-hostile websites simply won’t be recommended. One thing ties all of this together: generic AI slop will get you nowhere in this world. Everyone can generate it, in infinite quantities, which makes it worth exactly nothing. The only thing that can’t be generated is being real: real work, real people, a real voice, real customers who really vouch for you. In an agent world, authenticity can’t just be claimed, it has to be checkable. An agent choosing between two suppliers can cross-reference everything, cheaply and instantly. Does the story on your site match the trail you leave everywhere else? Do the people actually exist? Do the reviews hold up? SEOs will recognize the goal: this is what Google’s E-E-A-T was always reaching for. The difference is that Google estimates trust from signals; an agent can simply go and check. Authenticity stops being a marketing word and becomes infrastructure: identity, provenance, trust. Proving you are who you say you are is about to become the most valuable layer of the web. You can’t build that layer on your own site alone: you also need to exist elsewhere on the web, and you need others vouching for you. Whether we call that link building, PR or something else doesn’t matter. It needs to be done. And the proof isn’t only technical: a genuinely good about page, real people with real faces and a real story, does the same work. It might be the best page on your site. Not dead. Not even close. But different: the website is becoming the verified, machine-readable home base of a business. It feeds every other surface. It answers agents and takes their orders. It proves the business is real. And it still, sometimes, gets visited by an actual human, who had better find something authentic when they arrive. The websites that thrive in this world get three things right. They’re technically excellent: now a choice, and for the moment still an edge. They’re easy for agents to find and read: a plumbing problem. And they’re unmistakably real: the hard part, and it always was.
